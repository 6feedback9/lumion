Why Google Isn't Indexing Your Pages: 4 Search Console Statuses and How to Fix Them

The Pages report in Search Console is full of confusing statuses. Here is what each one means, what to do and in which order.

First: what indexing means

Google first discovers a URL, then crawls it, and only then decides whether to add it to its index. Pages outside the index cannot appear in search, however well optimised they are. You can check the status in Google Search Console → Pages.

Status 1. "Discovered – currently not indexed"

Google knows the URL but has not visited it yet. Usually the page does not look like a priority: few internal links, a new or large site, or a slow server.

  • Link to the page from the homepage, menu or related articles
  • Make sure it is listed in sitemap.xml
  • Check server response time

Status 2. "Crawled – currently not indexed"

Google visited and read the page but decided it was not worth indexing. This is a quality signal: thin or templated text, similarity to other pages, little value for the reader.

  • Expand the content: answers to specific questions, examples, prices, tables
  • Merge similar pages into one stronger page
  • Add internal links from relevant pages

Status 3. "Alternate page with proper canonical tag"

Not always an error. Google sees several URLs with the same content and indexes the one you declared as primary. Make sure the canonical points to the URL you want in search, and that the sitemap lists only canonical URLs (no http/https, www or trailing-slash duplicates).

Status 4. "Excluded by noindex tag"

Fine for privacy policy, terms and cookies pages. A problem if noindex ends up on service pages or articles. Check the page code and CMS settings.

A real example

In our own Search Console report on October 23, 2026, 44 pages were indexed and 51 were not, mostly statuses 1–3. We are now fixing internal links, canonicals and the sitemap and expanding the content. This is a typical picture for a young site.

What to do, in order

  • Open Pages and read the reasons for non-indexing
  • Fix technical causes (noindex, canonical, sitemap)
  • Strengthen internal linking to important pages
  • Improve the content of weak pages
  • Request reindexing with URL Inspection

FAQ

Why is Google not indexing my site?

The most common causes: few internal links, thin or duplicate content, canonical or noindex mistakes, and a young domain.

How long does indexing of a new page take?

From a few days to a few weeks. Submitting the URL via URL Inspection and linking to it from the homepage speeds it up.

Is it normal that not every page is indexed?

Yes. Google does not index every URL, but your key service pages and articles should be.
